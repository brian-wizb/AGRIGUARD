Smallholder farmers of Mchicha and Spinach in Tanzania lack a fast, reliable and low-cost way to 
diagnose crop diseases and pest infestations. Existing options—self-diagnosis, waiting for 
extension officers, or consulting social media groups—are slow, error-prone or unverified, 
resulting in poor treatment decisions, reduced yields and unnecessary chemical expenditure. 
While computer vision offers a proven route to accurate leaf-based diagnosis, most existing mobile 
agricultural applications stop at providing a diagnostic notification and do not connect that 
diagnosis to any physical response in the field. This leaves a gap between software-level insight 
and real-world pest control action. 
Furthermore, farmers require an interface that is simple enough to operate on an ordinary 
smartphone without technical expertise, and that can communicate diagnostic results in a form 
they can immediately act on. There is therefore a need for a mobile-based software subsystem 
capable of detecting and classifying crop diseases from images, presenting this information 
through a farmer-friendly interface, and automatically driving a physical pest-control response 
through a reliable serial data link to companion hardware. 
1.3. PROJECT OBJECTIVE 
1.3.1. GENERAL OBJECTIVE 
The main objective of this study is to develop a machine learning-based smart system that 
automates plant disease detection and pest control management to improve crop protection for 
smallholder farmers. 
1.3.2. SPECIFIC OBJECTIVE 
To achieve the general objective, the project focuses on the following specific milestones: 
2i. To develop a machine learning subsystem for crop disease detection. 
ii. To develop a machine learning subsystem for crop disease and pest classification. 
iii. To develop a mobile-based subsystem for user interaction. 
iv. To implement a Serial Communication module in the software application that has 
command to the Arduino-based hardware trap.
